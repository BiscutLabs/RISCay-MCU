# Native SRAM clock candidate

A4 is a proposed replacement for the per-byte clocked boundary. Production
still drives the three fixed GF180 SRAM macros from the service clock. The
reviewed preflight supports building an isolated native byte owner; it does
not qualify a production replacement.

Each variant should retain one native word request, a lane counter and assembled
read data. Its byte owner supplies one complete, locally timed macro clock pulse
per enabled lane. No periodic clock is needed to advance that sequencing.
The macro CLK remains a real clock pin with pulse, input and output timing
obligations, even when its edges are generated by a native handshake.

## Fixed macro evidence

The eleven assets in `soc/sram-lock.json` are unchanged. The five pinned Liberty
corners supply setup/hold for all A/D/CEN/GWEN/WEN inputs, minimum high/low/period,
CLK-to-Q and load/slew constraints. The preflight and fresh review reproduce
560 input timing reports and 80 output timing reports, plus 45 deliberate
violations at their intended checks. These are tool-binding checks at specified
loads and slew, not measurements of a native controller.

The Liberty bus timing covers all ten address bits. Its `memory.address_width`
metadata nevertheless says nine, and the supplied Verilog omits A[9]'s setup
check. Retain those discrepancies and monitor the actual pins independently.
Do not silently alter the fixed macro views.

Full-macro SPICE has not produced a functional measurement. The first attempt
failed model selection; a documented compatibility-mode retry timed out. Fresh
review found that both decks also held CEN active from startup, unlike the
behavioral initialization and production sequence. A new deck starts CEN high
and lowers it with legal setup; that bounded retry also timed out with zero
measurements. These are incomplete experiments, not observed SRAM failures.

Use the pinned Liberty as the declared black-box timing contract for the
prototype while retaining its provenance limitations. A hypothetical maximum
stopped-clock interval or an arbitrary supply collapse is not a reason to add
periodic stages or postpone every native experiment. Macro extraction,
characterization provenance, rail/variation and whole-chip signoff remain open
for both the existing boundary and its proposed replacement.

## Byte ownership and completion

Admission captures and retains address, data and enables. Its acknowledgment
must have a precise meaning: ownership transfer, not an unproved memory effect.
The owner then establishes setup at the macro pins, generates a single rising
edge, maintains minimum high time and input hold, captures read data after
CLK-to-Q plus routing/setup, and completes low-time/period obligations before
the next edge. A write has no DONE output, so effect completion needs a bounded
macro timing contract; Q activity cannot prove it, since repeated reads may
return identical values.

All inequalities use actual macro and receiver pins, both transition polarities,
every supported lane/mask and all pinned corners. Include earliest control
against latest data, fanout, routing and reset interaction. A simulation delay
or the mapper's generic delay-cell floor alone is not this proof. Keep failed
setup/hold, pulse, slew and load checks visible.

BD must complete independent request/ACK return-to-zero. Click must retain its
own byte and word phases; neither lane parity nor a four-phase adapter can stand
in for that history. Backpressure holds the result with CLK low and CEN inactive.
An idle request or disabled lane must not produce a CEN glitch or clock pulse.

## Reset is the new integration obligation

At a valid rail, a stop/reset request closes admission at an explicitly defined
closure point. No new byte is admitted after closure; an already committed byte
may complete afterward under the reviewed effect rule. Closure must not truncate
its pulse or release its held macro inputs.
The byte owner acknowledges quiescence only after the required high, hold,
capture, low and return obligations finish. Resetting the upstream word owner
before its accepted byte has captured its payload is also forbidden. Source
payload and protocol history remain intact until joined quiescence. Both owners
participate in closure; stale architectural replies are suppressed.

The isolated prototype may distinguish `bootstrapReset` from `stop` and
`quiescent`. The generated chip wrapper already distinguishes power-good and
manual reset, but the portable SoC currently receives their combined qualified
reset. Production integration must carry the necessary distinction internally
or prove an equivalent local startup/stop circuit. Connecting combined POR
directly to the pulse owner's ordinary asynchronous reset can make a runt pulse.
No production port, reset policy or accepted-effect rule changes in this
preflight.

Cold power loss remains a separate destructive event outside the declared
operating envelope; no orderly hold-up guarantee is established. No digital
circuit guarantees completion under arbitrary rail collapse.
Application watchdog reset continues to preserve SRAM, loader accounting, image,
lock and accepted effects. Sleep begins only after committed work and returns
drain. The existing rule for interrupted POR writes must be retained explicitly,
including which byte is irrevocably committed when stop and launch overlap.

## Next experiment and evidence

Build separate BD/Click byte-owner fixtures using public primitives and the
fixed macro interface. Cover legal source reuse, repeated values, every byte
mask, read/write alternation, stalled replies, stop at every ownership/pulse
boundary and repeated stop/rearm. Assert actual-pin setup/hold, high/low/period,
capture and idle CEN behavior. Mutants that truncate a pulse, reset pins early,
double-clock or capture Q early must fail at their intended assertion sites.
Map that exact circuit and measure its complete min/max envelopes before
replacing the production boundary. Word sequencing, loader/firmware, retention,
sleep and strict-export tests follow at integration.

Evidence: `build/async-sram-preflight/preflight-r1.json` and
`independent-review-r1/seal.json`. The fresh review binds the corrected CEN
experiment, independently replayed STA reports and exact plan corrections.
No production area, leakage or wake-edge reduction is claimed for A4 yet.

## Launch versus stop arbitration

The existing public Mutex model can support an isolated digital owner now.
It arbitrates persistent launch/stop requests without converting the Click
payload channel. The MCU mapper lacks its physical recipe, and the native
inventory does not yet classify it; neither fact demonstrates clocked storage.
A production binding needs the separate
[reusable primitive proposal](mutex-physical-binding-proposal.md).

The owner must observe grant low before reusing a contender, and clear/rebase
old requests before electrical reset release. Mutex has no epoch identity: a
request still high after reset can legitimately win again. Reset/rearm policy
belongs to the owner. An unresolved competition leaves CLK low and CEN inactive;
there is no deterministic maximum physical arbitration resolution time.

Fresh review reproduces seven finite model probes and two exact wiring controls,
plus two independent rearm witnesses, under
`build/async-sram-byte-prototype/independent-review-r1/seal-r2.json`. The
grant-only probes contain no byte owner, retained payload or SRAM-pin evidence.
They do not qualify the missing physical arbitration circuit. The corrected
proposal permits separate native digital byte-owner development while that
physical work remains open; it does not authorize production replacement.
